What the research says

Suite is priced at the market rate for email alone. At 10,000 contacts we are against the median and cheaper than of the products that publish a price there. A buyer is not paying a premium for the wallet channel; they are paying roughly what Mailchimp costs and getting it.

Suite gets expensive on large lists. At 100,000 contacts we run above the median, because our line is close to linear while the field discounts steeply. Large-list Suite deals have to be won on the on-chain capability or quoted specially. This is the same structural fact as on the Send side, and it is the strongest argument for revisiting the Suite curve before chasing enterprise logos.

Send still undercuts the market badly — just not EmailOctopus at the top. against the median at 10,000 subscribers. Against the specialists that price near cost, we now sit above them past the crossover; against the incumbents a buyer actually shortlists, we remain far cheaper.

We are narrower than the incumbents in one place only. We score of 11 on capability. SMS is the single thing we do not sell at all, and ecommerce is the only shallow one. Everything else in this market we ship, including a transactional API and the contact record and journey view that ActiveCampaign and Brevo charge for as a CRM — ours is Audience.

Nothing in this field has a wallet channel. All ladders price one thing: how many people you can email. Zero of them price in-app wallet messaging, on-chain triggers or wallet enrichment. That is not a gap we found in their pricing — it is a capability none of them has, and it is the only durable reason Suite exists.

Below 2,500

EmailOctopus is free to subscribers and emails a month. Under that size the competing price is zero, not a discount, so the bottom rung of Send converts on product and deliverability — never on price. Judge it on conversion, not margin.

Why fixed plus a rate, and not a curve

Every competitor prices email on a sublinear power curve, price ∝ subscriberse, with e below 1 — each extra thousand subscribers costs the customer less than the last. EmailOctopus measures e =  across our range ?.

A base fee plus a flat rate is not a power curve, but across the range we actually sell it behaves like one: its effective exponent is between 1,000 and 100,000 subscribers ?. It buys the shape of a discount curve while keeping our own arithmetic linear, which is the point: our cost is linear in volume, so a linear price keeps margin from decaying. Margin settles at instead of sliding, so the plan needs no cap.

Against EmailOctopus we are at 5,000 subscribers and at 100,000: the two lines . Below the crossover we win on price; above it we do not. That was accepted when Send was repriced on 24 Aug: at the corrected email cost, price leadership at the top of the range and a healthy margin at scale could not both be had.

Margin runs at 1,000 subscribers down to at 100,000 — the opposite way round to a flat rate, where the smallest accounts were the worst. The base fee is what fixed that: card fees take of a 1,000-subscriber bill now, against a quarter of it before.

Costs not yet in the model

  • Storage growth. Every other cost stops when a customer stops. Contacts, wallets, event history and automation logs only grow, including for dormant accounts. Needs a retention policy before the data exists.

Ceiling cost and floor profit

The rule. A plan’s price must exceed the cost of its own full allowance. If that holds, the plan cannot lose money at any usage level.

Ceiling cost is a customer consuming every unit of every allowance, plus dedicated IP, bundled concierge and the card fee. Floor profit is price minus ceiling cost.

Why overage is the strongest revenue line

How to retire one

  • Churn needs three months of cohort data. Until then every LTV figure on page 06 is arithmetic on a guess, and the CAC ceiling that follows from it should not be spent against.
  • Credits per wallet is now measured — 1,000 enrichments billed 2,950 credits, about 3 per wallet. Wallets per contact is still an instrumentation job, not an analysis job. It can be answered in a week by logging what enrichment actually consumes.
  • Utilisation comes free once billing runs: it is consumed units over allowance, per account, per month.
  • The infrastructure base is now part-itemised on page 03 from the provider price sheet. The remainder has no invoice behind it and is the largest single unexplained number in the model. Reading one month of invoices closes it.
  • Overage rate cannot be measured until customers have exceeded an allowance, which cannot happen until allowances are live. It is modelled at deliberately, so the projection never claims credit for revenue the document argues is our strongest line.
